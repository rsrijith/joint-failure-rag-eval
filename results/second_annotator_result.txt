A second human annotator (no involvement in operator design) labeled a blind,

citation_relocation). Same rubric and item format as the first annotator. Packet:
validation/second_annotator/. Scored by scripts/second_annotator_score.py.

Agreement with the FIRST annotator:

  Task A paraphrase equivalence     : 23/25 agree (92%)
  Combined balanced faithfulness gold (50, =25 should-be-faithful + 25 should-be-

Interpretation:
- The combined kappa 0.920 is the headline (balanced classes; directly comparable to
  the human-vs-Mistral kappa = 0.891 already reported). Two independent humans agree
  slightly MORE than human-vs-model.
- Task-A kappa in isolation is degenerate (near-uniform "equivalent" marginals; the
  kappa paradox), so we report Task-A raw agreement (92%), not its kappa.
- The 2 Task-A disagreements are borderline paraphrase calls, NOT faithfulness errors:
  PN-029 (annotator 2 accepted a typo normalization "commission"->"commitment" as
  meaning-preserving; annotator 1 did not) and PN-097 (annotator 2 flagged an added
  "Unlike the EU" clause as a content change; annotator 1 accepted it). Both are
  Task-A equivalence judgments; Task-B faithfulness agreement was perfect.

This closes the single-annotator limitation: the validity gold is now corroborated by
a second INDEPENDENT human, not only a model proxy.
